About This Project

Teryaq is a graduation project that uses an AI ensemble (ResNet50 + ConvNeXt-Tiny) to identify 25 medicinal and toxic plant species from a photo, with a full platform around it: encyclopedia, comparison, mood-based suggestions, and a database-grounded chat assistant.

Safety Guide

This is not a medical tool

Predictions and plant information are for educational purposes only. Never use this app to decide whether a plant is safe to eat, touch, or use medicinally.

Understand the confidence levels

"Confirmed" means the model is reasonably sure. "Uncertain" or "Unmatched" means the photo didn't clearly match one of the 25 trained species — treat those results as unreliable, not as a real identification.

Poisonous ≠ obvious

Some toxic plants (like Oleander) closely resemble harmless ones in photos. Never touch, taste, or let children or pets near an unidentified plant based on this app's output alone.

In a real emergency

If someone may have ingested a poisonous plant, contact your local poison control center or emergency services immediately — don't wait on an app.

        How the model works

        This system uses an ensemble of two convolutional neural networks trained on 25 medicinal and toxic plant species.

        ResNet50
        Classic deep residual network (2015), 23.5M parameters.
        ConvNeXt-Tiny
        Modern (2022) architecture inspired by transformers.
        Ensemble weight
        60% ResNet50 + 40% ConvNeXt-Tiny
        Test accuracy
        97.05%

        Each model was trained in two phases — feature extraction on a frozen pretrained backbone, then fine-tuning the last layers at a low learning rate. Grad-CAM (Selvaraju et al., 2017) is used on ResNet50's last convolutional block to visualize which regions of the image most influenced each prediction.
